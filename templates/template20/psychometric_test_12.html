Motivation Assessment

Full Description

Motivation assessment evaluates the underlying drives that energize and direct human behaviour toward goals. It primarily distinguishes between intrinsic motivation—engaging in activities for inherent satisfaction, curiosity, and personal fulfilment—and extrinsic motivation—performing behaviours to obtain external rewards like money, grades, or recognition, or to avoid punishments.

Personality Analysis

Personality Analysis - HEXACO Model

Our assessment is based on the comprehensive HEXACO personality framework, which evaluates six major dimensions of personality:

Honesty-Humility

Measures sincerity, fairness, greed avoidance, and modesty. High scorers are genuine, unassuming, and fair-minded, while low scorers may be manipulative, boastful, or greedy.

Emotionality

Assesses emotional reactivity, anxiety, dependence, and sentimentality. High scorers are emotionally sensitive and empathetic, while low scorers are emotionally stable and tough-minded.

Extraversion

Evaluates social boldness, sociability, liveliness, and expressiveness. High scorers are outgoing, energetic, and confident in social situations, while low scorers prefer solitude and are more reserved.

Agreeableness

Measures forgiveness, gentleness, flexibility, and patience. High scorers are cooperative, tolerant, and trusting, while low scorers tend to be critical, stubborn, and competitive.

Conscientiousness

Assesses organization, diligence, perfectionism, and prudence. High scorers are disciplined, careful, and hardworking, while low scorers are more flexible but less organized.

Openness to Experience

Evaluates aesthetic appreciation, inquisitiveness, creativity, and unconventionality. High scorers are imaginative, curious, and open to new ideas, while low scorers prefer conventional approaches.

Motivation Analysis

Motivation Assessment

Our comprehensive motivation evaluation distinguishes between two fundamental types of motivation that drive human behavior and achievement:

Intrinsic Motivation

Internal drives that come from personal satisfaction and fulfillment:

  • Curiosity and Learning - Desire to understand and explore new concepts
  • Personal Growth - Drive for self-improvement and skill development
  • Autonomy - Need for independence and self-direction
  • Mastery - Pursuit of excellence and competence
  • Purpose - Connection to meaningful goals and values

Extrinsic Motivation

External factors that influence behavior and decision-making:

  • Rewards and Recognition - Response to praise, grades, and achievements
  • Social Approval - Desire for acceptance and status
  • Competition - Drive to outperform others
  • Avoidance of Consequences - Motivation to prevent negative outcomes
  • Financial Incentives - Response to monetary rewards and security
Career Interest Analysis

Career Interest Inventory - RIASEC Model

Our assessment is based on John Holland's renowned RIASEC model, which categorizes personalities into six distinct types:

Realistic (Doers)

Practical, hands-on individuals who enjoy working with tools, machines, and physical tasks. Suited for engineering, agriculture, construction, and technical fields.

Examples: Engineering, Construction, Mechanics

Investigative (Thinkers)

Analytical, research-oriented people who enjoy solving complex problems and exploring ideas. Ideal for science, research, medicine, and technology careers.

Examples: Research, Science, Technology

Artistic (Creators)

Creative, expressive individuals who value aesthetics and self-expression. Perfect for arts, design, writing, music, and media careers.

Examples: Design, Media, Fine Arts

Social (Helpers)

People-oriented individuals who enjoy helping, teaching, and working with others. Suited for education, healthcare, counseling, and social services.

Examples: Teaching, Healthcare, Counseling

Enterprising (Persuaders)

Leadership-oriented people who enjoy persuading, managing, and taking risks. Ideal for business, sales, politics, and entrepreneurship.

Examples: Business, Sales, Leadership

Conventional (Organizers)

Detail-oriented individuals who prefer structured, organized work environments. Perfect for accounting, administration, banking, and data management.

Examples: Finance, Administration

Cognitive Assessment

Aptitude Assessment

Our comprehensive aptitude evaluation measures seven key cognitive domains essential for academic and career success:

Logical Reasoning

Problem-solving abilities, critical thinking, and the capacity to analyze complex situations and draw logical conclusions.

Numerical Reasoning

Mathematical abilities including arithmetic, algebra, data interpretation, and quantitative problem-solving skills.

Mechanical Reasoning

Understanding of physical principles, mechanical concepts, and how machines and systems work.

Spatial Reasoning

Ability to visualize objects in space, understand 3D relationships, and mentally manipulate shapes and forms.

Abstract Reasoning

Pattern recognition, conceptual thinking, and the ability to identify relationships between abstract concepts.

Clerical Reasoning

Attention to detail, accuracy in routine tasks, and ability to process information systematically.

Language/Verbal Reasoning

Communication skills, vocabulary, reading comprehension, and verbal expression abilities.
