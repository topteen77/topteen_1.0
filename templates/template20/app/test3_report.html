INTELLIGENCE ASSESSMENT REPORT

An intelligence test is a standardized assessment designed to measure a person's cognitive abilities and intellectual potential. These tests evaluate various factors of intelligence to provide a comprehensive understanding of an individual's mental capabilities. The key factors often assessed in an intelligence test include numerical reasoning, verbal reasoning, logical reasoning, mechanical reasoning, spatial reasoning, language usage and spelling, and critical reasoning. By identifying your dominant Reasoning type, you can better understand your career preferences and suggest suitable career paths that align with your strengths and passions.

Reasoning type description
Reasoning Type Description
Numerical Reasoning
Ability to understand, interpret, and work with numerical concepts and relationships. It involves the capacity to reason with numbers and perform mathematical operations to solve problems.
Verbal Reasoning
Ability to understand, analyze, and interpret written and spoken language. It involves evaluating information and arguments presented in words to make logical conclusions.
Logical Reasoning
Ability to analyze and evaluate arguments, identify patterns, and solve problems using logic. It involves deducing conclusions from premises and understanding the principles of logic.
Mechanical Reasoning
Ability to analyze and evaluate arguments, identify patterns, and solve problems using logic. It involves deducing conclusions from premises and understanding the principles of logic.
Spatial Reasoning
Ability to visualize and manipulate objects in space. It involves understanding spatial relationships and properties of objects.
Language Usage and Spelling
Ability to understand and use language correctly, including grammar, punctuation, and spelling. It involves the accurate and effective use of words in communication.
Critical Reasoning
Ability to analyze, evaluate and synthesize information objectively and systematically. It involves reasoned judgements, problem-solving ability and capability to form well-supported conclusions along with recognizing biases and considering alternative perspective.
