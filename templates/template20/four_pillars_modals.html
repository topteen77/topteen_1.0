Building Your Support Network

Family Support System

Help Your Family Understand Your Four Pillars:

  • Share your assessment results and explain what they mean
  • Ask for support in your strength areas and patience in challenging areas
  • Request help in finding opportunities that match your interests
  • Communicate your goals and how they can help you achieve them
Teacher and Mentor Relationships

Building Strong Relationships:

  • Share your Four Pillars profile with teachers in your strength areas
  • Ask for additional challenges and opportunities in subjects you love
  • Seek extra help in challenging areas, showing your commitment to improvement
  • Find mentors who work in fields related to your interests
Peer Network Development

Finding Your Tribe:

  • Connect with students who share your interests
  • Form study groups with complementary strengths
  • Join clubs and activities where you can meet like-minded peers
  • Be open to friendships with people who have different strengths
Professional Network Building

Starting Early:

  • Attend career fairs and professional events
  • Join professional organizations that have student memberships
  • Connect with professionals on LinkedIn (with parent permission)
  • Participate in industry-related competitions and events
College And Career Planning With The Four Pillars
College Selection Strategy

Choose Colleges That Support Your Four Pillars:

Academic Programs:

  • Look for strong programs in your Natural Ability areas
  • Ensure they offer learning environments that match your Learning Preferences
  • Check if they have opportunities related to your Interest Drivers

Campus Culture:

  • Visit campuses to see if the environment matches your Engagement Pattern
  • Talk to current students who share your interests
  • Evaluate extracurricular opportunities

Support Services:

  • Look for academic support that matches your learning style
  • Check for career services in your interest areas
  • Evaluate mentorship and networking opportunities
Career Exploration Framework

Use Your Four Pillars to Explore Careers:

Step 1: Identify Career Clusters — Based on your strongest pillars, research career clusters that align with your profile.

Step 2: Information Interviews — Connect with professionals in fields that interest you. Ask about:

  • Daily responsibilities and challenges
  • Required skills and education
  • Career advancement opportunities
  • Work environment and culture

Step 3: Job Shadowing and Internships — Gain firsthand experience in potential career areas:

  • Observe professionals in action
  • Try tasks related to the field
  • Network with industry professionals
  • Confirm or adjust your career interests

Step 4: Skill Gap Analysis — Compare your current abilities with career requirements:

  • What skills do you need to develop?
  • What education or training is required?
  • How can you gain relevant experience?
  • What timeline is realistic for your goals?

Special Strategies for Common Challenges
When Your Pillars Don't Seem to Align

Sometimes students discover that their Four Pillars seem to conflict. This is actually normal and can lead to unique opportunities.

Example Conflict Scenarios:

Scenario 1: The Analytical Creative

  • Natural Ability: Strong in both Math/Science AND Creative Arts
  • Challenge: Feeling torn between "logical" and "creative" paths
  • Solution: Explore fields that combine both (graphic design, architecture, digital animation, game design, biomedical illustration)

Scenario 2: The Social Introvert

  • Learning Preference: Social Learning (works well in groups)
  • Engagement Pattern: Knowledge Seeker (prefers independent research)
  • Solution: Find small, focused study groups; become the "research expert" in group projects; pursue online collaborative learning

Scenario 3: The Practical Theorist

  • Natural Ability: Strong in abstract subjects like Philosophy or Literature
  • Interest Driver: Hands-on Technical Crafts
  • Solution: Explore applied philosophy (ethics in engineering), technical writing, or teaching technical subjects
Overcoming Academic Struggles in Non-Strength Areas

When You Must Take Courses Outside Your Strengths:

Strategy 1: Connect to Your Interests

  • Find ways to relate required subjects to your passion areas
  • Example: If you love music but struggle with math, explore the mathematical relationships in music theory

Strategy 2: Use Your Learning Preference

  • Apply your natural learning style to challenging subjects
  • Example: If you're a hands-on learner struggling with history, create timelines, build models, or visit historical sites

Strategy 3: Find Study Partners

  • Team up with students whose strengths complement yours
  • Teach them in your strong areas, learn from them in your challenging areas

Strategy 4: Seek Alternative Resources

  • Use videos, apps, games, or other media that present information in ways that match your learning style
  • Find tutors who understand different learning approaches
Building Confidence in Your Unique Profile

Remember:

  • There is no "perfect" or "best" combination of pillars
  • Your unique profile is your competitive advantage
  • Many successful people have unconventional combinations
  • Diversity of strengths makes teams and communities stronger

Confidence-Building Activities:

  • Keep a "success journal" documenting your achievements
  • Seek feedback from teachers and mentors about your strengths
  • Find role models who share aspects of your profile
  • Celebrate small wins and progress, not just major achievements
