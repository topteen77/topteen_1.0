CAREER INTEREST ASSESSMENT REPORT

This report is based on the RIASEC model, developed by John Holland, which categorizes career interests into six distinct types: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, and Conventional. By identifying your dominant interest types, you can better understand your career preferences and suggest suitable career paths that align with your strengths and passions.

Holland’s Code description
Holland Code Description

Realistic
Individuals with Realistic interests prefer hands-on activities and practical tasks. They enjoy working with tools, machines, or animals & often excel in environments that require physical activity & problem-solving. Careers that suit Realistic types include engineering, construction, agriculture, and skilled trades.

Investigative
Investigative individuals are curious and analytical, often drawn to research and intellectual challenges. They enjoy exploring ideas, conducting experiments, and solving complex problems. Suitable careers include those in science, medicine, technology, and academia, where critical thinking and data analysis are paramount.

Artistic
Artistic individuals thrive in creative and expressive environments. They enjoy activities that involve art, music, writing, and design, often seeking opportunities to innovate and express themselves. Careers in the arts, entertainment, fashion, and media are ideal for Artistic types, as they allow for personal expression and creativity.

Social
Social individuals are empathetic and enjoy helping others. They excel in roles that involve teaching, counseling, and providing support. Careers in education, healthcare, social work, and human resources are well-suited for Social types, where they can make a positive impact on people's lives.

Enterprising
Enterprising individuals are natural leaders and persuaders. They enjoy activities that involve leading teams, making decisions, and taking risks. They often thrive in dynamic environments where they can influence others and achieve goals. Careers in business, management, sales, and entrepreneurship are ideal for Enterprising types, who are driven by ambition and leadership opportunities.

Conventional
Conventional individuals prefer structured and orderly tasks. They excel in roles that involve organization, data management, and attention to detail. Careers in accounting, administration, finance, and logistics suit Conventional types, where precision and reliability is the key.

PERSONALITY ASSESSMENT REPORT

Personality assessment is the process of measuring and evaluating individual personality traits, behaviors, and characteristics. This assessment helps in understanding an individual's consistent patterns of thinking, feeling, and behaving.  This report is based on the Big Five personality traits (OCEAN) representing a widely accepted framework for understanding human personality. These traits are Openness to Experience, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism.

Openness to Experience
Conscientiousness

Reasoning type description
Personality Code Characteristics
Openness to Experience (O) Openness to experience involves the degree to which a person is imaginative, curious, and open-minded. People high in this trait tend to be adventurous and appreciate art, new ideas, and novel experiences. Those low in openness may prefer routine and familiarity. Suitable careers include those in creative fields like art, music, writing, and research roles that require innovation and abstract thinking.
Conscientiousness (C) Conscientiousness reflects how organized, dependable, and disciplined an individual is. Highly conscientious people are often goal-oriented, mindful of details, and reliable. Those with low conscientiousness may be more spontaneous and less focused on long-term goals. Careers in management, administration, accounting, and healthcare can be a good fit, where attention to detail, reliability, and organization are crucial..
Extraversion (E) Extraversion measures the extent to which a person is outgoing, energetic, and enjoys social interactions. Extraverts tend to be enthusiastic and seek out company, whereas introverts may prefer solitude and quiet activities. Roles in sales, public relations, teaching, and event planning can be ideal for extraverts, who thrive in social and dynamic environments.
Agreeableness (A) Agreeableness describes how cooperative, compassionate, and friendly an individual is. Those high in agreeableness are often empathetic and enjoy helping others, while those low in this trait may be more competitive and less concerned with others' feelings. Careers in social work, counseling, customer service, and healthcare are well-suited for individuals high in agreeableness, where empathy and collaboration are key.
Neuroticism (N) Neuroticism refers to the tendency to experience negative emotions like anxiety, depression, and irritability. High neuroticism indicates a person may be more sensitive to stress, while low neuroticism suggests a more emotionally stable and resilient individual. Careers in roles that provide stability and support, such as administrative positions or technical roles, can be beneficial for individuals with higher neuroticism, helping them manage stress effectively.
