Chapter 1: Introduction to Interviews and Professional Etiquette

 

Section 1: Why Interview Skills Matter for Teens

What’s the Big Deal About Interviews? Interviews might seem like something far off in the future, reserved for adults in stiff suits. But guess what? They’re already a part of your life, or they will be very soon. An interview is essentially a conversation where you get to showcase who you are, what you can do, and why you’re the best fit for an opportunity. For high school students, interviews pop up in several exciting (and sometimes nerve-wracking) situations:

  • College Admissions: Many competitive colleges, especially Ivy League schools or specialized programs, require interviews. This is your chance to show your personality, passion, and fit for the school—beyond what’s on your transcript.
  • Internships: Whether it’s a summer gig at a tech company, a local nonprofit, or even a creative role like photography, internships often involve interviews to see if you’re eager and capable.
  • Part-Time Jobs: From flipping burgers to babysitting, employers use interviews to gauge if you’re reliable, friendly, and a good match for their team.
  • Scholarship Opportunities: Some scholarships require interviews to decide who gets the funding. Your ability to communicate your story can literally pay off!

Why Start Building These Skills Now? Think of interview skills as a superpower you can develop over time. The earlier you start, the more natural they’ll feel when the stakes are high. Mastering these skills now means you’ll walk into any room with confidence, whether it’s for a school presentation or a future job interview. Plus, these skills spill over into everyday life—helping you ace group discussions, stand out in class, or even convince your parents to let you borrow the car (we’ve all been there!).

Real-Life Example: Let’s talk about Carlos, a high school junior who dreamed of attending a prestigious art school. He had a killer portfolio, but his interview skills were shaky. After practicing with a teacher, he learned to talk about his artwork with passion and explain how it reflected his identity. During the college interview, he connected with the interviewer over a shared love for street art. That personal touch, combined with his confidence, helped him secure a spot—and a partial scholarship!

Fun Fact for Teens: Did you know that some of your favorite celebrities, like Billie Eilish or Timothée Chalamet, had to “interview” for their first big roles? Even Hollywood stars need to impress directors and producers with their communication skills!

Interactive Element – Role-Play Challenge: Pair up with a friend or family member and pretend you’re interviewing for your dream part-time job (maybe at a gaming store or a pet shop). Take turns being the interviewer and the candidate. Notice how you feel—do you stumble over words or forget to smile? Write down one thing you did well and one thing you want to improve. This mini-practice will prep you for the real deal!

Think About It (Expanded): What’s one opportunity (like a job, internship, college, or scholarship) you’re excited about in the next year or two? How do you think interview skills could help you land it? Also, consider a time when you had to “sell yourself”—maybe for a school project or club role. How did it go, and what would you do differently now? Jot down your thoughts—we’ll revisit them as we progress through the course.

 

Section 2: What Is Professional Etiquette?

Breaking It Down: What’s Professional Etiquette? Professional etiquette might sound like a boring, old-school term, but it’s really just about knowing how to act in situations where first impressions and respect matter. It’s the set of behaviors and manners that show you’re mature, dependable, and considerate—whether you’re in a classroom, at a job, or in a formal meeting. As a high schooler, you’re already navigating environments where these skills come into play, and mastering them now will make you stand out in a big way.

Key Aspects of Professional Etiquette : Here are the core elements of professional etiquette, tailored to situations you might encounter as a teen, along with tips to make them second nature:

  • Communication: Speak clearly, avoid slang in formal settings, and use a friendly but respectful tone. For instance, if you’re emailing a teacher about a missed assignment, don’t write, “Hey, what’s good with that homework?” Instead, try, “Dear Mr. Smith, I’m sorry I missed the assignment. Could I have an extension?”
    • Tip: Practice by reading your emails or texts out loud before sending them. Does it sound polite?
  • Punctuality: Being on time shows you respect others’ schedules. If you’re late to a team practice, it can throw off the whole group. Imagine being late to a job interview—employers might think you’re not serious.
    • Tip: Set reminders on your phone for important events, and aim to arrive 5-10 minutes early.
  • Appearance: Dressing appropriately shows you care about the situation. You don’t need expensive clothes, but showing up to a school career day in pajamas won’t scream “I’m ready for the future.” Opt for clean, neat outfits that match the vibe of the event.
    • Tip: Keep a “go-to” outfit ready for unexpected formal events—think nice jeans, a collared shirt, or a simple dress.
  • Respect: Listen actively, don’t interrupt, and use polite words like “please” and “thank you.” Holding the door for someone or thanking a teacher after class are small acts that go a long way.
    • Tip: Make it a habit to say “thank you” at least five times a day for a week. You’ll be surprised how natural it feels!
  • Digital Etiquette: In today’s world, how you act online matters too. Avoid posting rude comments on social media, and don’t text during meetings or interviews. If you’re on a Zoom call for a virtual interview, keep your background tidy and mute yourself when not speaking.
    • Tip: Double-check your social media profiles. Would you be okay with a college admissions officer or employer seeing your posts?

Why Does It Matter for High Schoolers? Professional etiquette isn’t just for corporate offices—it’s for building trust and credibility wherever you go. Teachers might write stronger recommendation letters if they see you as respectful. Future bosses will notice if you’re the employee who’s always polite to customers. Plus, these habits prepare you for bigger moments, like networking with professionals or representing your school at events. Starting now means you’ll be ahead of the game when you step into college or the workforce.

Relatable Scenario: Meet Priya, a high school senior working at a local bookstore. One busy day, a frustrated customer couldn’t find a specific book. Instead of getting annoyed, Priya calmly offered to check the inventory system and even called another store to locate it. She thanked the customer for their patience. The customer later left a glowing review online, and Priya’s manager gave her a shout-out at the next staff meeting. That’s professional etiquette turning a tough moment into a win!

Fun Challenge for Teens: Try the “Politeness Experiment” for three days. Say “please” and “thank you” every chance you get, even for tiny things like someone passing you a pencil. Also, practice active listening—when someone talks to you, nod or say “I see” to show you’re engaged. Keep a mini-journal of how people react and how it makes you feel. Do you notice a difference in how others treat you? Do you feel more confident or connected? Share your findings with a friend or in class!

Think About It : Have you ever been in a situation where someone’s behaviour (good or bad) stood out to you? Maybe a classmate was super rude during a group project, or a cashier was extra kind despite a long line. How did their actions affect you, and what can you learn from it about professional etiquette? Also, think about a time you could’ve handled a situation more professionally—how would you do it differently now?

 

Section 3: Setting Personal Goals for the Course

Why Set Goals? Learning interview skills and professional etiquette is like training for a sport or mastering a video game—it takes practice, focus, and a clear idea of what you’re aiming for. Setting personal goals helps you pinpoint exactly what you want to improve, whether it’s overcoming shyness, sounding more polished, or just feeling less awkward in formal settings. Goals give you direction and motivation, turning this course into a personal growth adventure tailored to you.

How to Set Your Goals : Let’s walk through a detailed 4-step process to set goals that are realistic, inspiring, and trackable:

  • Reflect on Yourself: Take a quiet moment to think about how you handle conversations, presentations, or formal situations. Are you shy and struggle to speak up? Do you talk too much and forget to listen? Are you great with words but freeze under pressure? Be brutally honest with yourself—it’s the first step to growth.
    • Reflection Prompt: Think of the last time you had to speak in front of others (like a class presentation or meeting a new person). What went well, and what felt off?
  • Pick Specific Areas to Improve: Narrow down 1-3 skills or behaviors you want to focus on. Avoid vague goals like “I want to be better at talking.” Instead, go for something specific like, “I want to stop fidgeting when I answer questions,” or “I want to make eye contact for at least 5 seconds when introducing myself.”
    • Tip: Start small. Pick goals you can realistically work on during this course.
  • Make It Measurable: Figure out how you’ll know if you’ve improved. For example, “By the end of this course, I want to answer at least two questions in every class discussion without hesitating,” or “I want to practice a 3-minute mock interview with a friend and get feedback on my tone.”
    • Tip: Use numbers or specific actions to track progress—it makes success feel tangible!
  • Connect It to Your Dreams: Tie your goal to a bigger purpose. Why does this matter to you? For instance, “I want to improve my public speaking because I’m applying to a leadership program next summer, and I need to stand out.” Linking your goal to a personal dream keeps you motivated.
    • Reflection Prompt: How will achieving this goal help you in the next 1-2 years?

Example Goals from Other Teens :

  • Mia: “I want to improve my handshake. Right now, it feels weak and awkward, and I want it to feel firm and confident by the end of the course. I’ll practice with friends and family until I get it right.”
  • Ethan: “I get super nervous before presentations or interviews. My goal is to learn breathing techniques to calm myself down and test them before my next class speech.”
  • Aisha: “I talk really fast when I’m excited or nervous. I want to slow down and speak clearly so people understand me better. I’ll record myself practicing answers and aim to reduce my speed by half.”
  • Jayden: “I’m terrible at remembering names when I meet new people. My goal is to learn a trick to recall names during conversations and use it at the next school event I attend.”
  • Lila: “I avoid asking questions because I’m scared of sounding dumb. My goal is to ask at least one question in every class or meeting this month to build my confidence.”

Fun Activity for Teens : Create a “Goal Vision Board” (digitally on apps like Canva or on paper with magazines and markers). Cut out or draw pictures that represent your goals—like a microphone for public speaking, a handshake for confidence, or a graduation cap for college dreams. Add a few words or quotes about why this goal matters to you. For an extra twist, include a “reward” image—like a pizza slice or a new game—to celebrate when you hit your goal. Hang it somewhere you’ll see it daily (like your bedroom wall or phone wallpaper) as a constant reminder to stay focused! If you’re comfortable, share a photo of your vision board with a friend or teacher for accountability.

Bonus Tip – Accountability Buddy: Team up with a classmate or friend taking this course. Share your goals with each other and check in weekly to see how you’re doing. Cheer each other on and swap tips—it’s more fun (and effective) when you’re not going at it alone!
