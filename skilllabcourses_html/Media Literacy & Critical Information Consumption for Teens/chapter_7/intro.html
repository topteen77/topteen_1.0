Chapter 7: News Literacy for Teens

Hey there, high school students! Welcome to Chapter 7, where we’re diving into the world of news and how to make sense of it in today’s fast-paced, info-overloaded digital age. As teens, you’re constantly bombarded with headlines and updates on social media, but how do you know what’s real or worth paying attention to? In this chapter, we’ll explore how news is created, how to pick trustworthy sources, and how to engage with it like a critical thinker. Let’s get started on becoming news-savvy!

  • How News Is Made and Distributed Ever wonder how a story goes from happening to hitting your phone as “breaking news”? We’ll walk through the news cycle, showing how events turn into headlines with 24/7 updates that can sometimes rush the facts. You’ll also learn the differences between local, national, and global news outlets—each with its own focus and spin. For teens who get most of their updates online, understanding this process will help you see why not every notification deserves a freakout.
  • Choosing Reliable News Sources Not all news is created equal. Some outlets are trusted and stick to the facts, while others are sensationalist clickbait or unverified blogs just chasing views. As a teen scrolling through feeds, it’s easy to fall for flashy headlines, so we’ll show you how to spot the good from the bad. Plus, we’ll introduce fact-checking tools like Snopes and FactCheck.org to help you double-check current events before believing or sharing them. Knowing where to get your news is half the battle!
  • Engaging with News Critically Clicking a headline isn’t enough—you’ve got to dig deeper for the full story. We’ll teach you how to read beyond the catchy titles to understand the real context, especially since snippets on social media can mislead. We’ll also talk about balancing how much news you consume so you don’t get overwhelmed or stuck in a bubble of bias. For teens trying to stay informed without losing their minds, these skills will help you process the world’s chaos smartly.

In this chapter, we’ll cover these topics and put your news skills to the test with a “News Comparison” activity, where you’ll compare two stories on the same event to spot differences in tone, facts, and bias. By the end, you’ll be a pro at navigating the news landscape, separating truth from spin, and staying informed without getting played. Let’s dive into becoming news-literate teens!
