Chapter 2: Understanding Personal Data and Privacy

 

Section 1: What is Personal Data? Identifying Sensitive Information

What’s the Deal with Personal Data? Personal data is basically any information that can point to YOU. It’s like your digital fingerprint—stuff that identifies who you are, where you are, or what you’re doing. Think of it as the puzzle pieces that, when put together, paint a picture of your life. Some of this info is obvious, like your name or phone number, but other bits might surprise you!

Examples of Personal Data:

  • The Obvious Stuff: Your full name, home address, phone number, email address, and school name. Example: Posting “Hey, I’m Jake from Lincoln High!” on a public forum gives away your name and school.
  • The Sneaky Stuff: Your location (like when you tag where you are on Instagram), your birthdate, or even your favourite hangout spots. Example: Tagging “Chilling at Starbucks on Main St.” tells people exactly where to find you right now.
  • The Super Sensitive Stuff: Your Social Security number (if you’ve got a job), bank account details, passwords, or even private messages. Example: Sharing a screenshot of a convo where you accidentally show your password—yikes, that’s a goldmine for hackers!

Why Should You Care? If someone gets hold of your personal data, they can pretend to be you (identity theft), stalk you, or scam your friends by using your info. Imagine someone hacking your account and messaging your bestie, “Hey, send me $20, I’m stuck!”—and they fall for it because they think it’s you.

Fun Fact for Teens: Did you know that even your gaming username could be personal data if it’s tied to your real name or email? Like, if your Fortnite handle is “JakeSmith2005,” someone might guess your name and birth year. Time to switch to something like “NinjaPotato” instead!

Think About It: Take a sec to list 3 pieces of personal data you’ve shared online recently (like your email on a sign-up form or a photo with your house in the background). Don’t worry, you don’t have to show anyone—just think about how easy it was to share that info without a second thought.

 

Section 2: Why Privacy Matters: Risks of Data Exposure

Privacy = Your Digital Shield Privacy is all about controlling who gets to see your personal data and how it’s used. Without privacy, you’re like a house with no locks—anyone can just walk in and take what they want. For teens, privacy isn’t just about hiding stuff; it’s about staying safe from creeps, scams, and even future problems (like a college or job seeing something embarrassing you posted years ago).

What Happens When Your Data Gets Exposed? Here are some real risks, with examples that hit close to home:

  • Cyberbullying Gets Personal: If someone knows your address or school from a post, they could use it to harass you IRL (in real life). Example: Sarah posted a pic of her new house with the street sign visible. A bully from school used it to send creepy messages like, “I know where you live.”
  • Identity Theft: Hackers can use your info to open accounts or buy stuff in your name. Example: Mike shared his birthdate and full name on a gaming forum. A scammer used it to guess security questions and access his email.
  • Stalking or Physical Danger: Oversharing location data can let strangers track you. Example: Emma always tags her location on Instagram Stories. A weird follower started showing up at her favourite café—she had no idea how they found her.
  • Future Consequences: Colleges, employers, or even scholarship programs might check your online presence. Old posts can haunt you! Example: Alex posted a rant about hating school at 14. At 17, a college admissions officer found it and questioned his “attitude” during an interview.

Cool Stat to Chew On: A 2022 study showed that 1 in 3 teens have had their personal info stolen online because of weak privacy habits. That’s a lot of us! Let’s not be part of that stat.

Challenge for You: Imagine you’re a hacker (just for fun!). Look at your own social media profile through their eyes. What info could you grab in 5 minutes? (Like your school from a tagged post or your pet’s name, which might be a password hint.) Jot down one thing you’d “steal” and think about hiding it better.

 

Section 3: Social Media and Privacy Settings: What You Need to Know

Social Media: Your Stage, Your Rules Social media is where most of us live online—Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, you name it. But every post, story, or DM is a chance for someone to peek into your life if you’re not careful. The good news? Privacy settings are like your personal bouncer—they decide who gets into your digital party and who’s stuck outside.

Key Privacy Settings to Master (Platform by Platform):

  • Instagram:
    • Set your account to “Private” so only approved followers see your posts (go to Settings &gt; Privacy &gt; Account Privacy).
    • Turn off “Location Services” for posts so your pics don’t show where you are.
    • Example: Mia set her account to private after random accounts started commenting weird stuff on her beach pics. Problem solved!
  • TikTok:
    • Make your account “Private” to control who sees your vids (Settings &gt; Privacy).
    • Disable “Suggest Your Account to Others” so strangers don’t find you easily.
    • Example: Jayden turned off comments on a viral dance vid after trolls started spamming hate. He still got views from friends but skipped the drama.
  • Snapchat:
    • Set “Who Can View My Story” to “My Friends” instead of “Everyone” (Settings &gt; Privacy Controls).
    • Turn off “Quick Add” so random people can’t add you.
    • Example: Lily stopped sharing her location on Snap Map after a creepy “friend of a friend” kept asking where she was.

Why Bother with Settings? Without them, your posts are like shouting in a crowded mall—everyone hears you, including people you don’t want listening. Plus, social media apps often default to “public” to get more users engaged, not to protect you. It’s on YOU to flip the switch.

Teen Hack Alert: Did you know some apps track your location even when you’re not using them? Check your phone’s “Location Services” (under Settings on iPhone/Android) and turn off tracking for apps like Snapchat when you’re not posting. Sneaky, right?

Try This Now: Pick one social media app you use most. Open it, go to Privacy Settings, and check if your account is public or private. If it’s public, switch it to private (or ask a parent/teacher if you’re unsure). Screenshot your updated setting as proof you’re a privacy pro!

 

Section 4: Safe Sharing: What to Post and What to Avoid Online

Sharing Smart, Not Hard Sharing online is fun—it’s how we connect, laugh, and show off our lives. But there’s a line between “cool post” and “way too much info.” Safe sharing means thinking twice before you hit “send” or “post.” Let’s break down what’s okay and what’s a no-go.

What’s Safe to Post (Green Light):

  • General vibes or hobbies: “Loving this new playlist!” or a pic of your art project (no personal details).
  • Stuff with friends’ permission: A group pic at the mall, but only if everyone in it is cool with being online.
  • Non-identifying content: A meme, a funny GIF, or a post about a game you’re into without tagging your location. Example: Noah posts a pic of his skateboard tricks but blurs out the park sign in the background. Smart move!

What to Avoid Posting (Red Light):

  • Full personal info: Your address, phone number, school schedule, or birthdate. Example: Don’t post “Just turned 16, party at 123 Oak Street tonight!”—that’s an invite for trouble.
  • Location tags in real-time: Tagging where you are right now tells everyone (including strangers) where to find you. Example: Posting “At the movies, row 5!” while you’re there is risky. Wait until you’re home to share.
  • Private convos or drama: Screenshots of texts or fights can backfire and hurt people (or get you in trouble). Example: Kayla shared a screenshot of a fight with her BFF. It spread, and now half the school’s involved. Ouch.
  • Anything you’d regret later: Rants, inappropriate pics, or stuff that might look bad to a future boss or college. Example: Tyler posted a joke about skipping class. His coach saw it and benched him for a game. Double ouch.

Golden Rule for Teens: If you wouldn’t yell it out loud in the school cafeteria for everyone to hear, don’t post it online. Once it’s out there, you can’t take it back—even if you delete it, someone might’ve saved a screenshot.
