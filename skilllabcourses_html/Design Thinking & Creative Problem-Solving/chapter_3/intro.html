Chapter 3: Defining the Problem – Finding the Root Cause

Introduction: Turning Insights into Focus

Welcome, high schoolers, to Chapter 3 of your Design Thinking adventure! After diving deep into empathy in Chapter 2 and learning how to understand others’ needs, it’s time to take all those observations, stories, and insights and transform them into something actionable. This chapter is all about Defining the Problem—the second stage of Design Thinking—where we shift from simply noticing issues to pinpointing the root causes and framing them in a way that sparks creative solutions.

Defining the problem is like setting up the target before you shoot an arrow. If your target is unclear or off-center, your solutions won’t hit the mark, no matter how brilliant they are. In this chapter, we’ll guide you through the process of moving from vague observations to focused problem statements that drive real change. You’ll learn how to ask powerful questions like “Why?” and “How Might We?”, narrow down broad issues into specific challenges, avoid jumping to conclusions by validating your ideas with peers, and write clear, user-centered problem statements. Whether you’re tackling a school issue, a community challenge, or a personal project, mastering this stage ensures you’re solving the right problem for the right people. So, let’s sharpen our focus and get ready to define problems that matter!
